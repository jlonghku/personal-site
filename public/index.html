Long Jiang

Ecohydrology, urban water systems, and intelligent environmental modelling

I study how water moves through cities, watersheds, and aquatic ecosystems, and how those systems respond to environmental change. My work combines process-based models, field observations, geospatial analysis, and machine learning to build useful and interpretable tools for water management.

Research

Understanding water systems across scales

My research centres on a practical question: how can physical understanding and data intelligence work together to improve environmental prediction and decision-making?

01

Intelligent ecohydrological modelling

Developing hybrid approaches that connect mechanistic representations of hydrology with deep learning. Current interests include knowledge distillation, model interpretability, and learning across spatial resolutions.

Methods: process-based models, PyTorch, knowledge distillation, cross-resolution learning

02

Urban flooding and drainage systems

Examining how drainage networks, land cover, and green infrastructure shape urban runoff and flood risk. The work connects hydrological connectivity with simulation and optimisation for more resilient cities.

Methods: SWMM, graph analysis, optimisation, geospatial analysis

03

Water quality and aquatic ecosystems

Investigating hydraulic, biochemical, and ecological responses to changing flow conditions in constructed wetlands, lakes, and other aquatic systems, informed by long-term monitoring and coupled models.

Methods: field monitoring, EFDC, OpenFOAM, sensitivity and uncertainty analysis

04

Environmental decision support

Building integrated modelling frameworks that translate climate, land-use, and population scenarios into evidence for watershed and ecosystem management.

Methods: VELMA, SWAT, scenario analysis, high-performance computing

Selected research programmes

Examples of the systems and scales represented in my work.

Watershed scale

Puget Sound Integrated Modelling Framework

Coupled atmosphere–land cover–marine modelling of streamflow, nitrogen dynamics, forest–hydrology interactions, and ecosystem outcomes across the Puget Sound basin.

Urban scale

Resilient urban drainage and flood modelling

Combining SWMM, graph theory, and optimisation to assess hydrological connectivity and improve drainage performance across urban green infrastructure systems.

Ecosystem scale

Constructed wetlands and aquatic systems

Using long-term monitoring and coupled hydraulic and water-quality models to understand aquatic-system responses under seasonal change and shock loading.
